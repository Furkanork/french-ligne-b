        Le passé

        The number-one B1 skill is telling a story. That means choosing between passé composé and imparfait. Think of a film: the imparfait is the set, the passé composé is the action.

        Imparfait · the set

        Background, habits, states, "was …-ing"

        Signal words: d'habitude usually, souvent often, tous les jours every day, autrefois in the old days, à l'époque back then, quand j'étais… when I was…, pendant que while

        Form: nous present minus -ons + ais, ais, ait, ions, iez, aient. Only one irregular: être → ét-.

        Passé composé · the action

        Events that happened, finished, moved the story on

        (closed period, even if long)

        Signal words: soudain / tout à coup suddenly, hier yesterday, un jour one day, une fois once, d'abord / ensuite / enfin first / then / finally, pendant + a fixed length for (two years…)

        Together

        The interruption pattern

        Ongoing action (imparfait) is cut by an event (passé composé). This one sentence shape covers a huge share of real stories.

        One step further back

        Plus-que-parfait = "had done"

        avais / étais + participle. Same choice of avoir/être as the passé composé.

        Les pronoms

        A2 speakers repeat nouns: "Tu as vu le film ? Oui, j'ai vu le film." (Did you see the film? Yes, I saw the film.) B1 speakers say Oui, je l'ai vu. (Yes, I saw it.) Five little words and one order chart make you sound fluent at once.

        Futur & si

        Plans, wishes, regrets, polite requests. Future and conditional share one stem, and si-sentences follow three fixed patterns.

        One stem, two tenses

        Stem = the infinitive (drop the final -e of -re)

        Futur: stem + ai, as, a, ons, ez, ont →
        Conditionnel: stem + imparfait endings ais, ais, ait, ions, iez, aient →

        The 12 irregular stems: être ser- · avoir aur- · aller ir- · faire fer- · pouvoir pourr- · vouloir voudr- · savoir saur- · venir viendr- · devoir devr- · voir verr- · envoyer enverr- · falloir faudr-

        The three si patterns

        Never put futur or conditionnel right after si

        Realsi + présent → futur
        Imaginedsi + imparfait → conditionnel
        Regretsi + plus-que-parfait → cond. passé
        Two traps English speakers fall into

        quand + futur

        English says "when I arrive". French uses the future:

        Politeness lives in the conditionnel

        Use the conjugator in station 1 to see futur, conditionnel and conditionnel passé for any verb.

        Le subjonctif

        It looks like a whole new world, but it's a reflex triggered by certain phrases. Learn the triggers, the one formation rule and eight irregular verbs.

        Triggers · after "que"

        Wanting, feeling, necessity, doubt

        • Want / ask: vouloir que want (someone) to, souhaiter que wish that, demander que ask that, préférer que prefer that
        • Feel: être content / triste / surpris que be glad / sad / surprised that, avoir peur que be afraid that, regretter que be sorry that
        • Must: il faut que (someone) has to, il est important / nécessaire / normal que it's important / necessary / normal that
        • Doubt / deny: douter que doubt that, ne pas penser que not think that, ne pas croire que not believe that
        • Conjunctions: bien que although, pour que / afin que so that, avant que before, sans que without, à moins que unless, jusqu'à ce que until
        Not triggers · indicative

        Certainty, hope, thinking (positive), "after"

        • penser que, croire que, trouver que (positive) think, believe, find that
        • espérer que hope that
        • il est sûr / certain / évident que it's sure / certain / obvious that
        • après que after, parce que because, pendant que while, alors que whereas, même si even if

        Same subject? Skip "que" and use the infinitive: not je veux que je parte.

        Formation

        Take the ils-form, drop -ent, add e, es, e, ions, iez, ent

        ils finissent →
        ils prennent →

        nous / vous look like the imparfait: que nous prenions. For -er verbs the singular sounds like the present.

        Irregular eight: être sois · avoir aie · aller aille · faire fasse · pouvoir puisse · savoir sache · vouloir veuille · falloir faille

        Reporting what people said, and the compact structures that make your French sound written-level. One tense-shift table covers reported speech.

        Reported speech · after "il a dit que"

        He said:He said that…

        Questions: yes/no → si; « Qu'est-ce que… ? » → ce que; « Qu'est-ce qui… ? » → ce qui. Time words shift too: demain → le lendemain tomorrow → the next day, hier → la veille yesterday → the day before, aujourd'hui → ce jour-là today → that day.

        Two actions at once, or "by doing"

        Form: nous-stem + ant (nous faisons → en faisant). Exceptions: étant, ayant, sachant.

        Boss B2

        The traps that still catch upper-intermediate learners. Each has a cheat code.

        Boss 1

        Participle agreement with avoir

        Sometimes the participle takes -e or -s even with avoir.

        Agree only when the direct object comes before the verb: You only hear it with participles ending in a consonant: écrite, prise, mise, faite.
        Boss 2

        C'est or il est?

        Both translate as "it is / he is".

        C'est + a determiner or name: Il est + a bare adjective or job: For a general remark:
        Boss 3

        depuis, il y a, pendant, dans, en

        All of them are "for / in / ago" in English.

        depuis + present = still going: · il y a = ago · pendant = a finished duration · dans = from now · en = time it takes:
        Boss 4

        Register

        Natives switch between three Frenches.

        familier slangcourant everydaysoutenu formal
        bookun bouquinun livreun ouvrage
        joble boulotle travaill'emploi
        carune bagnoleune voitureun véhicule
        manun mecun hommeun individu
        Speak courant, understand familier, write soutenu for exams and work.
        Boss 5

        The "ne" that isn't negative

        A ne appears with no pas, and nothing is negated.

        After avant que, à moins que, avoir peur que and comparisons, formal French adds an empty ne: Ignore it when reading; you never have to say it.
        Boss 6

        B2 false friends

        They look easy, so you don't check them.

        éventuellement = possibly · finalement = eventually · assister à = to attend · prétendre = to claim · supporter = to tolerate · une déception = a disappointment · achever = to finish · sensible = sensitive

        Atelier DELF

        Whether or not you sit the exam, the DELF tasks are the best definition of "B level": tell, explain, argue. Write here, and the B-mètre shows which B-level structures you used.

        The exams at a glance
        DELF B1DELF B2
        Structure4 parts, 25 points each: listening, reading, writing, speaking. Pass = 50/100 with at least 5/25 in every part.
        Writing~160 words: a personal letter, forum post or article giving your opinion~250 words: an argued text (essay, formal letter, article)
        SpeakingGuided interview, a role-play, then giving your view on a short documentPresent and defend a viewpoint from a short document, then debate it with the examiner
        Key skillTell, describe, give reasonsArgue for and against, nuance, convince
        Essay skeleton (B2)

        Four blocks, every time

        1. Introduction: set the topic, ask the question, announce the plan.
        2. Part 1: two arguments, each with an example.
        3. Part 2: the other side, or limits.
        4. Conclusion: your answer, then a wider question.

          The other 80%

          Grammar is the 20%. Hours of French are the rest.

          Grammar gets you to B1 on paper. What gets you there in real life is time spent with French. A simple weekly plan:

          • Listen daily, 20–40 min, to French made for learners and then for natives: RFI's Journal en français facile, the InnerFrench podcast, TV5Monde's learning site, then French YouTube and series with French subtitles.
          • One station a week. Do the drill, then write 5 sentences of your own using that structure.
          • Shadow 5 minutes a day: replay a sentence and say it along with the speaker.
          • Speak once a week with a tutor or a language exchange partner, and use the opinion kit from station 6.
          • Want proof of your level? The DELF B1 / B2 are the official exams.
